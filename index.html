Health Science

The rice science
no one told you.

Why aged Sona Masoori, multigrain blends, and protein rice are among the healthiest grain choices you can make.

Can You Eat Rice on a Diet? Yes — If It's Aged.

The blanket advice to "cut rice" confuses all rice with fresh, high-starch, polished white rice. Aged Sona Masoori is chemically different. After 12–24 months of natural aging, starch crystallises into resistant starch — a form your digestive system cannot break down as easily.

The result? Lower blood glucose response. Slower digestion. Less insulin demand. The same grain that dietitians told you to avoid actually becomes diet-compatible when properly aged.

The Science

Resistant starch acts more like dietary fibre than regular starch. Studies show it produces a 20–30% lower postprandial glucose response compared to equivalent fresh rice. This is why aged basmati has been promoted for decades — now Grainary brings the same benefit to South Indian Sona Masoori.

Why Multigrain? The 5-Grain Advantage.

Our Multigrain Rice Blend combines Sona Masoori with four millet varieties chosen specifically for their nutritional synergy:

The 5 Grains

1. Sona Masoori (Aged) — Low GI base. Familiar South Indian texture.
2. Brown Rice — High fibre, bran layer intact, Vitamin B6 and magnesium.
3. Foxtail Millet (Thinai) — GI of ~50. High iron and calcium. Digestive support.
4. Barnyard Millet (Kuthiraivali) — Highest fibre content. Supports weight management.
5. Kodo Millet (Varagu) — Rich in antioxidants. Hypoglycemic properties for diabetics.

The combination creates a meal that is lower GI than any single grain alone, higher in fibre, richer in micronutrients, and — critically — still delicious enough for South Indian daily cooking.

Protein Rice — Why RNR/Jeera Sona?

Rice is not a high-protein food, but it is not protein-free either. Our Protein Rice variety — RNR/Jeera Sona aged 18+ months — has approximately 8.5g of protein per 100g of dry grain, compared to 6.8g in standard Sona Masoori. The difference matters at scale: a typical 200g cooked serving delivers 17g of protein — meaningful when combined with dal, curd, or eggs.

The aging process concentrates protein naturally by reducing moisture content. No fortification, no additives — just time and patience.

Best for Fitness-Conscious Households

If you train, track macros, or simply want more protein without switching entirely to quinoa or chicken breast, Protein Rice RNR is the South Indian kitchen's answer. Pair with rajma, chole, or egg curry for a complete amino acid profile.
